# Universal LED Strip Remote Privacy Policy

<p class="maj">Last updated: October 9, 2026</p>

Universal LED Strip Remote is an Android app to control LED strips, published by **{{ site.editeur }}** ({{ site.pays }}). The data controller is **{{ site.responsable }}**, reachable at **[{{ site.contact }}](mailto:{{ site.contact }})**.

**In short:** no account, no ads, no analytics, no server. Everything you save stays on your phone. The app only talks to your strips: over Bluetooth, on your local Wi-Fi or by infrared.

## 1. What stays on your phone

The app stores **only on your device**:

- the strips you add (the name you give them, their type, the Bluetooth or local network address of their controller, the codes of their infrared remote);
- your rooms and which strips are in them;
- the last state sent to each strip (on or off, color, effect), because these controllers don't report it back.

This data is **never sent** to the publisher or to any server, and is not backed up to the cloud (Android auto backup is turned off for the app). It is deleted when you uninstall the app or clear its data in Android settings.

## 2. Bluetooth

The app looks for LED strip controllers around you when you ask it to ("Search around me" button), then connects to them to send your commands. It only reads the name and address of Bluetooth devices in range, to recognize the controllers. It **does not use Bluetooth to locate you**. On Android 11 and older, Android requires the location permission to scan for Bluetooth devices: the app asks for it for that reason only and never reads your location.

## 3. Local Wi-Fi

To recognize Wi-Fi controllers, the app sends short discovery messages **on your local network** and listens for their replies (name, model, local address). These exchanges stay on your network: nothing goes through the internet.

## 4. Infrared

If your phone has an IR blaster, the app uses it to send remote-control signals to your strips. No data is received this way.

## 5. Links to other apps

When the app recognizes a controller it can't control, it may offer to open the Google Play page of its original app. Google Play then opens, with its own privacy policy.

## 6. Shortcuts

The app accepts links (for example to turn a room on) that a shortcut or automation app can open on your phone. These links only turn your strips on or off; they give access to no data.

## 7. Crash reports

If you agreed to share diagnostic data with Google, Android and Google Play may send the publisher **anonymous** crash reports (phone model, Android version, where the crash happened) through the Google Play console. They are used only to fix bugs.

## 8. Android permissions

The app asks for: Bluetooth (find and control strips), location on Android 11 and older only (required by Android for Bluetooth, never used to locate you), Wi-Fi state and local network messages (recognize Wi-Fi controllers), network access, the IR blaster and vibration (touch feedback). It doesn't access your contacts, microphone, camera or files.

## 9. Why this data (legal bases)

| Use | Legal basis (GDPR) |
|---|---|
| Strips, rooms, strip state, Bluetooth and local Wi-Fi search | Running the app you asked for (performance of a contract) |
| Crash reports | Legitimate interest in fixing bugs |

## 10. Children

The app is meant for people aged 13 and over. It collects no data, and therefore no data from children.

## 11. Your rights

Since the data stays on your phone, you control it directly: to erase it, use "Forget this strip", delete a room, uninstall the app or clear its data.

Under the GDPR, you have the right to access, rectify, erase, object and restrict processing. For any question: **[{{ site.contact }}](mailto:{{ site.contact }})**. You can also lodge a complaint with the French data protection authority, the **CNIL** ([cnil.fr](https://www.cnil.fr/en/)).

## 12. Changes

This policy may change with the app. The date at the top shows the latest version; for any important change, the app will let you know.
